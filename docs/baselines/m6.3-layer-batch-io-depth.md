# #99 m6.3: a layer's misses in one drain, and two reads in flight per drive

Machine: 32 threads, 93 GiB RAM, 24 GiB VRAM, Linux 7.2.11. Drives: nvme0n1 = Samsung 9100 PRO (btrfs `compress=zstd:3` on LUKS, `/home`), nvme1n1 = Samsung 990 PRO (xfs, `/mnt/ssd1`); `max_sectors_kb` 128 on both. Model: GLM-5.3-Flash UD-IQ3_XXS, 4 split shards, one replica per drive (`manifest.json`, `O_DIRECT`). Measured 2026-11-01.

Cold = every shard of both replicas evicted with `scripts/evict-model-cache.py` (`POSIX_FADV_DONTNEED`; `drop_caches` needs a password here). Expert reads are `O_DIRECT` either way, so cold differs from warm only in dense-weight page cache.

## Changes measured

- `ExpertPool::ensure_batch` claims every missing unit of the layer in miss order, then sends all of their planes to the queue as one batched read (one drain) instead of one drain per unit.
- `golem_store::DEFAULT_IO_DEPTH` = 2 reader threads per drive (was 1).

## fio (`scripts/fio-qd-curve.sh`, 4812 KiB random reads, io_uring, one job)

| drive | QD1 MB/s | QD2 | QD4 | QD8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nvme0n1 (9100 PRO) | 8443 | 11943 | 11926 | 11793 |
| nvme1n1 (990 PRO) | 6278 | 7502 | 7502 | 7501 |

QD2 saturates both drives. Their sum, about 19.4 GB/s, is the ceiling for the pair.

## Shared queue (`read_bench`, 4.93 MB planes, 1200 reads, fresh seed per run)

`--batch 24` is one decode layer's misses for GLM (8 routed × 3 planes). Three repetitions, the second in reverse order:

| io_depth | rep 1 GB/s | rep 2 | rep 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 14.54 | 14.58 | 14.65 |
| 2 | **17.64** | **17.63** | **17.59** |
| 3 | 17.03 | 17.01 | 17.03 |

Batch 1 vs 8 at depth 1, 240 reads, both orders: 6.48–6.79 vs 13.58–13.75 GB/s. A one-unit drain leaves the drives half idle. `/proc/diskstats` confirmed the bytes came from the media (2.96 GB logical → 1.82 GB nvme0n1 + 1.14 GB nvme1n1).

## Decode (`scripts/ab-bench.sh`, 63 GiB pool, 128 tokens, ctx 1024)

Disk per run: 83.7 GB cold, 76.1 GB warm, identical across legs. Greedy output was byte-identical in every leg.

Batched `ensure_batch` vs the serial one (`HEAD` 204c93d), both at depth 1, order base → new → new → base:

| leg | tok/s cold | tok/s warm |
|---|---|---|
| base (A1, B2) | 6.571, 6.558 | 6.568, 6.571 |
| new (A2, B1) | 6.615, 6.539 | 6.622, 6.610 |

On its own, batching is within noise for decode. At a warm 63 GiB pool a token reads a median of 0.22 GB, so a layer call usually carries 0–2 missing units. Batching only pays where many units miss together: the first tokens (0.8–1.0 GB each) and prefill.

io_depth 1 vs 2 with the batched pager, order d1 → d2 → d2 → d1:

| io_depth | tok/s cold | tok/s warm | p3 wait ms/token | disk GB/s during the wait |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6.571, 6.547 | 6.647, 6.590 | 49–51 | 4.85–5.02 |
| 2 | **6.748, 6.748** | **6.741, 6.765** | 47–48 | 5.21–5.26 |

Depth 2 wins in all four pairs: leg means 6.559 → 6.748 tok/s cold (+2.9%), 6.619 → 6.753 warm (+2.0%).

## Prefill (`--prefill-stream`, ~1.8k-token prompt, 16 generated, 63 GiB pool)

| leg | io_depth | cold s | warm s | disk GB cold / warm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 1 | 55.8 | 44.6 | 130.5 / 116.4 |
| A2 | 2 | 51.2 | 44.4 | 124.1 / 116.4 |
| B1 | 2 | 50.4 | 44.1 | 124.1 / 116.4 |
| B2 | 1 | 49.5 | 45.9 | 123.9 / 116.6 |

Warm: depth 2 is 0.2–1.8 s faster. Cold shows no depth-2 win: the clean depth-1 leg (B2, 49.5 s) beats both depth-2 legs (51.2, 50.4 s). A1 read 6 GB more than the rest, so its 55.8 s is not comparable. Prefill streams about 2.6 GB/s here, well under the queue ceiling, so it is not disk-bound at this prompt size and depth does not move it.

## Profiles saved before this change

`golem optimize` stores io_depth only when it differs from the default. A profile from an earlier build where depth 1 won stored nothing, so it now runs at depth 2. Re-run `golem optimize` to re-price it, or set `io_depth = 1` explicitly.

## Why two threads per drive regressed before and help now

Design §2.4 cites 0.70 GB/s for two reader threads per drive at M3.3. That source (`docs/baselines/m3.3-store-bench.md`) is no longer in the tree, so the setup cannot be replayed. What has changed since: every chunk read is now `O_DIRECT` into an aligned destination, and workers self-select chunks served by their home drive (§5.2). Each worker issues one blocking `pread` per chunk, so the number of reader threads per drive is that drive's queue depth. fio shows QD2 is where both drives saturate, and the queue tracks it: depth 3 loses to depth 2 (`read_bench` above). A per-thread second read (io_uring) would reach the same QD2 without a second thread. At 1–2 threads per drive the thread cost does not show up in these numbers, so it was not built.

Unverified: whether btrfs `compress=zstd` applies to these shards. `compsize` needs root. `O_DIRECT` on compressed extents falls back to buffered I/O inside the kernel, and that would be one explanation for the old regression on nvme0n1.

## Arithmetic against the target

GLM-5.3-Flash UD-IQ3_XXS at a 63 GiB pool: 0.25 GB/token mean disk (`ab-report.py`; median 0.22) → 0.25 / 4.9 GB/s ≈ 51 ms at depth 1 vs 0.25 / 5.25 ≈ 48 ms at depth 2, of about 150 ms per token. The other ≈100 ms is compute and H2D (#100). During decode the in-wait rate (5.2 GB/s) stays far below the 17.6 GB/s queue ceiling, because per-layer miss lists are short. Decode is bounded by miss-list size and by the serial wait, not by drive depth.
