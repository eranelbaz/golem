# M3.7 evidence: policy replay, VRAM order, P5 prediction (issue #51)

Machine: RX 7900 XTX (gfx1100), ROCm 7.2.4, 24 GiB VRAM, 93 GiB RAM, nvme0n1 +
nvme1n1 replicas. Model: unsloth GLM-5.3-Flash UD-Q4_K_XL, revision
`621d456e`, 6 shards (shard-1 sha256 `00dceaf3…`): 42 MoE layers × 288
experts × 3 planes, unit 15.2–18.4 MB, Σ unit bytes 644,022,272 B/layer-set.

## 1. Routing traces

Four real prompts, 512 generated tokens each (511 decode steps), captured with the fixed trace ring
(`trace_caps`: `(n_ctx + n_predict) × n_layers` records — the previous
`n_predict + 256` cap kept only 6.9 decode steps of the M3.8 traces):

```

  --ram-experts-gib 59 --manifest <manifest> --ctx 2048 --n-predict 512 \
  --routing-trace rtN.tsv --timing-csv tmN.csv --prompt "<prompt N>"
```

| trace | prompt |
|---|---|

| rt2 | how a CPU executes an instruction, fetch to retirement |
| rt3 | short story: a lighthouse keeper and the sea |
| rt4 | two-trains word problem, then the general method |

Resolved device cache K = 18 slots/layer (printed by `golem run`), so prefill

## 2. Replay

`cargo run --release -p xtask -- replay --model <shard-1> --k 18 rt1.tsv … rt4.tsv`
(`xtask/src/replay.rs`). It mirrors production:

- **VRAM:** per-layer K-slot slab (P2). The victim is picked among the layer's
  non-routed slots. LRU and decayed-score metadata are booked on decode steps
  only (§6.2).
- **RAM:** one pool split into per-unit-size classes, `⌊pool × layers_c / Σ⌋`
  slots (`class_slots`, shared with `p3_glue::class_layout`). It sees VRAM
  misses only for residency; its policy metadata (CLOCK bits aside) is booked
  on every routed key. Units claimed by the current compute are leased for
  the whole compute.
- **CLOCK** sets the reference bit on reuse only, as `residency.rs` does.

d = routed expert bytes read from disk ÷ all routed expert bytes, decode steps
only. Production refuses † pools at K = 18 (floor = layers × K + 1 slots per
class); the replay simulates them anyway.

Geometry: 42 expert layers × 288 experts, 172.9 GiB of expert units; RAM classes 15216640 B × 39, 16330752 B × 2, 18427904 B × 1; VRAM K = 18 slots/layer. † = below the production pool floor (layers × K + 1 slots per class) at this K: simulated, but `golem run` refuses it.

## 3. Decisions

- **Scorer (§6.3 gate): keep it, at the VRAM tier only.** The tool's
  "max over pools" line includes the refused 10 GiB† pool; over runnable
  pools (40, 63 GiB):
  - VRAM: decay 44.2% hit vs LRU 42.0 (+2.2) and vs CLOCK 43.1 (+1.1). d at
    63 GiB: 18.0 (LRU or CLOCK) → 16.7% (−7% disk bytes); at 40 GiB 29.8 →
    27.0%.
  - RAM (decay vs CLOCK, VRAM LRU): +3.2 points of RAM hit at 40 GiB, but
    only +0.4 at 63 GiB (69.5 vs 69.1) — inside ≈1 point, so CLOCK stays.
  - Decay at both tiers under inclusive residency is worse than RAM CLOCK +
    VRAM decay (d 17.8 vs 16.7% at 63 GiB).
  - n = 4 traces, pooled; no per-trace spread is reported, so sub-point
    differences are not significant.
  - Shipped as the P2 evict-first order `--vram-order score` (default;
    `recency` kept for A/B). See §4 for the measured effect.
- **`Policy::pass` (P23) stays without a production consumer.**
  `--vram-order score` consumes its §6.2 score math (`decay_factor`,
  `HALF_LIFE_STEPS`). `pass()` models a global VRAM cap with background
  promotion; production VRAM is per-layer K slots with forced demand
  admission.
- **Exclusive residency (#64): the gate passes for pools ≤ ≈74 GiB.** With
  decay at both tiers, exclusive residency gives d 13.8% at 63 GiB vs 18.0%
  today (−23% disk bytes), and 23.8% vs 29.8% at 40 GiB. Exclusive with CLOCK
  RAM gains 1.1 points at 63 GiB (16.9%) and 3.3 at 40 GiB (26.5%); with CLOCK
  it loses at large pools (9.6 vs 9.4% at 90 GiB, 5.1 vs 4.6% at 120). #64
  should pair it with a decayed RAM tier and re-check the large-pool end.
- **Background VRAM promotion (#51 item 3): not built.** The user ruled on
  2026-10-07 from this table. Score-driven promotion with the 20% swap margin
  lowers VRAM hit rate (44.2 → 43.9% at 8/step, 43.1% at 32/step) and moves
  no disk bytes.
- **Routing profile (#51 item 2): deferred.** The user ruled on 2026-10-07.
  - Leave-one-out pre-fill leaves decode d unchanged (18.0 → 17.9% at
    63 GiB). Over the first 32 steps it is 19.4 → 18.5%.
  - It cuts prefill disk bytes 42.1 → 33.9%, but pre-filling a 59 GiB pool
    reads 63 GB before the first token.
  - #49 prefill streaming bypasses the pool for prefill, so revisit after it.
- **Fidelity:** not validated. At 63 GiB the replay predicts d = 18.0%
  (19.4% over the first 32 steps); `docs/baselines/m3.14-pool-size-baseline.md`
  measured 16.6% at 63 GiB on a different prompt — same ballpark, one point,
  not a calibration.
- **K sweep:** VRAM-infeasible beyond K = 18. K = 36 needs 36 × 42 × 15.2 MB
  ≈ 23 GB of slots, K = 72 ≈ 46 GB, against 24 GiB total with the dense
  weights resident. It bounds the VRAM tier's leverage: doubling K moves d by
  0.2 points.
- **Biggest lever:** pool size. d is 29.8% at 40 GiB, 18.0% at 63 and 9.4% at
  90 (cold, current policies).

Reading:
- Run-to-run tok/s spread within one setting is up to 0.4 tok/s (≈20%),
  larger than every A/B delta: **no tok/s effect is resolved** for any
  mode. Disk bytes are deterministic and are the decisive metric.
- `--vram-order score`: −3.4% disk bytes/token (1.16 → 1.12), matching the
  replay's direction (§3). Kept as default.
- `protect`: no disk change (1.12 → 1.13). Kept opt-in.
- `read`: +52% disk bytes/token at lookahead 1 (+68% at 2), more than half
  of the prefetched bytes wasted, no measurable speed-up. Kept opt-in, off
  by default.
- Predictor recall 83.8% (lookahead 1) / 87.2% (lookahead 2) of routed ids.
